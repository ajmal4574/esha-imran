Kathina Ceremony

Kathina is a Buddhist festival celebrated primarily in Theravada Buddhist countries, such as Thailand, Myanmar, Sri Lanka, and Cambodia. It typically occurs in the month following the end of the three-month Buddhist Lent, known as Vassa.
The Kathina ceremony involves the offering of robes and other requisites to Buddhist monks who have completed the Vassa retreat. Laypeople donate cloth and other necessities to the monks, who then use the donated materials to make new robes. These robes are then formally offered to the monks in a ceremony attended by lay devotees.

Kathina is considered a meritorious act for lay Buddhists, and it is often accompanied by various religious activities, including meditation, chanting, and almsgiving. The festival fosters a sense of community and generosity among both monastic and lay practitioners, strengthening their bonds and commitment to the Buddhist path.



History

Kathina is an ancient Buddhist tradition originating from the time of the Buddha. It was established as a way for laypeople to show gratitude and support to monks who had completed the rainy season retreat (Vassa). The offering of robes and other requisites during the Kathina ceremony symbolizes generosity and strengthens the relationship between monastic and lay communities.
