Eid Al-Adha

The history of Eid al-Adha traces back to the story of Prophet Ibrahim (Abraham) in Islamic tradition. According to the Quran and Hadith (sayings and actions of the Prophet Muhammad), Ibrahim was commanded by God in a dream to sacrifice his son, Isma'il (Ishmael), as an act of obedience and devotion.
Despite the difficulty of the task, Ibrahim resolved to fulfill God's command. As he prepared to sacrifice his son, God intervened and provided a ram to sacrifice instead, symbolizing Ibrahim's unwavering faith and submission to God's will.
This event is seen as a test of Ibrahim's faith and devotion, as well as a demonstration of the concept of total submission to God's commands in Islam. It underscores the importance of obedience, sacrifice, and trust in God's providence.
Eid al-Adha commemorates Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice and the ultimate provision of a substitute sacrifice by God. It is celebrated annually on the 10th day of Dhu al-Hijjah, the 12th month of the Islamic lunar calendar, following the culmination of the Hajj pilgrimage to Mecca.
Over the centuries, Eid al-Adha has become a significant religious and cultural festival for Muslims worldwide, marked by prayers, feasting, charity, and the symbolic act of sacrificing an animal as an offering to God. It serves as a reminder of the importance of faith, compassion, and generosity in Islam.
