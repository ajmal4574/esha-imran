Songkran

Songkran is the traditional Thai New Year's festival, celebrated annually from April 13th to 15th. It's characterized by water fights and splashing, symbolizing the washing away of the previous year's misfortunes and the welcoming of the new year with freshness and purity. Songkran also involves visiting temples to make merit, paying respects to elders by pouring scented water over their hands, and participating in lively street parades. It's a time for family reunions, cultural festivities, and joyous community gatherings throughout Thailand.



History

Songkran originates from ancient traditions in Southeast Asia, particularly in Thailand, where it is celebrated as the traditional New Year's festival. The word "Songkran" comes from the Sanskrit language, meaning "astrological passage" or "transformation." Historically, Songkran was originally a time for Thai people to visit temples to offer food to monks, clean Buddha images, and sprinkle water on the hands of elders as a gesture of respect and blessing. Over time, the festival has evolved into a vibrant celebration marked by water fights and street parties, symbolizing the cleansing of the old year and welcoming the new year with joy and purification.
